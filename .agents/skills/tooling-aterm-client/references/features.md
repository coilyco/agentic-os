# Features

A coarse inventory of what ships. Architecture: [architecture.md](architecture.md).

* **Seat overview** - where the app rests with a host attached and no seat open (COI-3518). Seats sort by what they want of Kai: Needs you (an open ask card or a waiting page, longest waiting first, with an Answer or Open word), Working, then Done (an unread reply first, with how long it has been so and Close where the seat is fully idle). The host is a label, never a group, and a heading shows only over seats. One line sits under a name: the question asked, else the seat's own activity line, else its last reply. A fault, a lock or a notice on the host still shows the host's full page. On a phone it is the first thing on screen, it is the seat list there (no second strip of seat chips), and the hosts, roles and switches sit under it (COI-3579). A launch returns to the host and rests here, and opens no seat, so a reload is one tap from the seat that was open. A logo button at the top opens the logo screen, the logo alone, and a tap or Escape returns. Grouping is `src/lib/overview.ts`. Not built: pins and the pinned last reply on a row (COI-3346, COI-3253).
* **Colour tokens (COI-3549)** - every colour in `src/` reads a token from the block in `src/app.css`, so a retune is one edit. The tokens are literal hex on purpose: a derived colour (`rgb(from var(--ground) ...)`) drifted one level in about 25 test bed screenshots, so a derivation belongs in a change that expects new pixels. xterm paints hex only, so `resolveColour` reads a token for it. The theme-color meta and the manifest are filled at build from `--ground` and `--plate`. The Android launcher ground is a resource file, so `os-chrome.test.ts` holds it to `--plate`. What each colour is for: [colour-meaning.md](colour-meaning.md).
* **Sidebar tabs** - hosts, running sessions, and roles to start as tab lists with arrow keys. Below 720px a seat row and a Menu button hold the seat first and put seats, hosts, and Settings one tap away.
* **Sessions as peers** - one tab per live session with its harness and code, so instances of a role on claude and codex sit side by side. A degraded startup names its skipped steps in the tab and header.
* **Live daemon** - "this Mac" speaks `aterm.daemon.v1` over the daemon's loopback websocket: roster, live sessions, attach with replay, typing, resize, and message states. A Demo host keeps a scripted copy for working with no daemon.
* **Host states** - checking, online, and not answering, each with its own panel and a retry. A host that answered before and goes quiet reads as stopped answering, not as never started.
* **Connection check** - a host that does not answer, or a link that stays down, runs a six-hop check on the host panel and names the hop that broke, saying which hops a page saw and which it could only infer. One button copies the report as a message to prod-director. [reach.md](reach.md).
* **Reconnect** - a daemon restart under an open window redials with backoff, keeps the open seat and roster greyed as last known, attaches seats again with replay, and offers a reload when the daemon came back as a newer build. A launch with the remembered host down selects it and attaches when it answers, with no tap (COI-3352). [architecture.md](architecture.md).
* **Seat launch** - any role launches on any of its harnesses through the daemon's `launch` frame, beside running instances. A refused or failed launch says which, with the daemon's reason.
* **Session terminal** - xterm.js, fit to its pane, with rows that touch so Claude Code's boxes stay solid (a taller row breaks box-drawing into dashes) and 10000 lines of scrollback. Cmd-click or Ctrl-click opens an http or https link in a new tab, and a plain tap does on a touch screen. Cmd+F, or Ctrl+Shift+F, opens find over the output, from the terminal or the composer: Enter for the next match, Shift+Enter for the previous, Esc to close. A Find button in the seat's header opens the same bar. Plain Ctrl+F stays the shell's (COI-3238).
* **Touch scrolling** - a finger drag scrolls the history on the normal screen, mouse reported or not. A full-screen app (alternate screen) has no history here, so the same drag becomes the wheel a mouse would send: wheel reports when the app tracks the mouse, arrow keys when it does not, and nothing at a bare prompt (COI-3537). Observed on the Android emulator, v0.36.0 and v0.37.0: no scroll in the alternate screen before the fix.
* **Jump to latest** - whenever the view sits above the newest output, a pill at the foot of the terminal says so, with a count (1 to 999+) of lines that arrived since the person scrolled away. One click or tap lands on the last line and focuses the terminal. At the bottom the view follows new output, typing returns to it, and a seat opens at its latest because each seat gets its own terminal. The pill waits while a choice card covers the foot of the terminal (COI-3253).
* **Quiet time on a row** - an idle seat's row says how long it has been quiet ("idle 12m"), a finished one how long Kai has been waited on ("done, your turn 3m"), and a working seat that has gone silent says so ("working, quiet 3m"), which is how to spot a stuck one. The time is the daemon's `quiet_seconds` backdated from when it arrived, moved forward by output this client sees, and refreshed by listing the seats every 20 seconds while the page shows. The daemon does not say when a state began, so a working seat that is writing shows no time (COI-3238).
* **Activity** - a seat's creature spins a ring in its colour while output flows, and a seat that finishes off screen shows "done, your turn" with a dot. It is not counted as waiting (COI-3342).
* **Close on a fully idle seat** - a Close button on the seat's row and in its own view, asked once ("Close it" or "Keep"), sent as `name@ref`. Offered only when the daemon reads the seat idle with `background` 0, no ask card or waiting page, no queued message, no draft and no unread reply. A daemon that sends no `background` offers none (COI-3488, COI-3491). A refusal for commits on no remote is said, and Close anyway sends the close again with `force`. Where the daemon has `reopen`, the list offers Reopen after a close and opens the seat under its fresh name.
* **Pin a seat** - a pin toggle on each row and in the open seat's header. Pinned seats lead the list and the phone's seat sheet. A person's pin or unpin is kept per device by session name and always beats a role's default. A role the roster marks `pinned_by_default` starts pinned (COI-3346, COI-3512).
* **Hide a role** - a "Seats by role" switch group in the menu, one per role with seats, on meaning shown, kept per device by role slug. A role switched off takes its quiet seats out of the list, and a note says how many are hidden with a Show them button. A hidden role's seat stays when it holds a question or a waiting page (the same set as the waiting count), is pinned, or is the open seat (COI-3346).
* **Where a seat started** - a small icon on each row and in the open seat's header: a terminal glyph for `origin: window`, a dashed box for `headless`, worded "started in a terminal window" and "started with no window" (never "app", since the daemon cannot tell). No `origin` shows nothing (COI-3504).
* **Waiting seats never move the screen** - waiting means a seat needs Kai: an open ask card, or a page it marked waiting (COI-3342). The window title, the app badge, the "N waiting" chip, the phone toast, the glow and the chime read that one list. The toast takes a tap only on its Open button, so the controls under it keep working (COI-3589). The title names the first, e.g. "(2) Frog-Ox asking // Treehouse". Nothing changes the selected seat except a click or tap: not returning to the tab, not answering a card, not a seat starting to wait (COI-2677). A pending ask takes the keyboard when a person opens its seat, number keys answer, and Enter submits a multi-select.
* **Alerts** - the Highlight is on by default and Sound is off, kept per browser, and a stored opt-out wins. Highlight draws a faint tint and a thin rail in each waiting seat's role colour until it is opened, with no motion and nothing at the window edge. The "?" or dot on the avatar and the brand-coloured name show regardless of the switch. On a desktop with hover, hovering or focusing a highlighted seat in the sidebar shows a preview with its identity, whether it is asking or done, and the question it asks (a finished seat shows only its state, since the client holds no text of its last reply). On a phone or touch screen a seat that starts waiting raises one toast under the seat bar, naming the first and counting the rest, that opens the seat when tapped and dismisses itself after 6 seconds. Neither the preview nor the toast takes focus. A cue fires when a seat enters waiting, so a seat already waiting at connect, the seat open on a visible tab, and asks a read-only browser cannot answer stay quiet. A browser holding audio back shows a notice on the Sound switch (COI-2491).
* **Notify when closed** - a third Alerts switch, off by default and shown only for a daemon that lists `web-push`. It subscribes this browser to the daemon's Web Push, so a seat that starts waiting notifies an app that is closed, and tapping the notification opens that seat. [web-push.md](web-push.md).
* **Settings** - one page for what is stored on this device (COI-3521), reached from a Settings button on the seat overview, the desktop sidebar's foot, the phone sheet's Hosts and settings section, and the host page's menu. On a phone it is the page, with no host menu above it. It holds the Alerts switches, Seats by role (hide a role's quiet seats, COI-3346), the view a new seat opens in (Auto, Focus, Default or Verbose, COI-3532), the terminal text size, the Wrap long lines default (Auto, On or Off, COI-3612), the hosts saved here with a Remove, the install card, and the Tap trace switch in the app. Each takes effect at once with no reload and applies to this device only. Back returns to what was open, and picking a seat, host or role closes it. The sidebar no longer holds the Alerts, Seats by role or Tap trace switches. Wrap itself stays a per-seat button in the terminal toolbar (COI-3544). Not here yet: the phone's return key choice (COI-3514) and the dictation engine (COI-3460).
* **Relayed questions** - a worker's `send_user_question` shows as a card on the director's pane, naming the worker, while both rows stay marked. The worker's own pane says where it went and offers a button to open it (COI-3241). Details in [choices.md](choices.md).
* **ask_choice** - a seat's structured `ask_choice` call shows as the same card, single or multi-select with an optional typed answer, and flags the seat "asking you" with a ? badge from any tab. A worker's `send_user_question` puts the same card on the director seat (`card_seat`), with the asker named and its own row still asking. The Demo host scripts two asks.
* **Native choices** - when a harness shows a select menu (Claude Code's AskUserQuestion, its trust prompt), a card offers the question and options as buttons, a typed answer for "Type something.", and Cancel. Picking one sends the harness its own keys.
* **Composer** - a real text field under the terminal, so dictation tools and phone keyboards can type into it. It is a plain text area with no combobox role, and Wispr Flow is unverified against it (COI-3254). Enter sends as one bracketed paste, with Enter a beat later, when the program asked for it. Typing `@` and a prefix offers the live sessions from the sidebar's list, with role and identity. Up and Down choose, Tab or Enter inserts the full name, Escape closes. The draft is kept per seat, outside the field, so a lock or seat change never takes it. Typed input the daemon would not take, or that met a closed socket, is said aloud above the field. While a seat works and the field is empty, Send becomes Stop in the same slot: a tap sends Escape, a touch hold sends Control-C, and Shift+Enter does from a keyboard. A draft keeps it Send. While a question card is open on the seat, Send waits and says so, since typed text would answer or dismiss the dialog, and the message stays as the draft until it is answered (COI-3322). It adds no daemon verb.
* **Images in the composer** - paste, drop, or pick up to four PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP images. Each shows as a chip with its size and a Remove button, and the Add image button is the way on a phone, a 44px target left of the field and centred with the field and Send (COI-3411). A still image over the host's 3 MB limit is shrunk in the browser first and the note says by how much, and a GIF over it is refused. Send uploads them one after another with `image_upload`, then pastes each returned path alone and the words after it, since a lone pasted path is what claude, codex and opencode attach ([image-upload.md](image-upload.md)). A failed upload sends nothing and keeps the draft. A goose seat attaches nothing, so the screen says it got the path. A host without the `image-upload` feature says so and shows no Add image button, so a paste is never dropped silently.
* **A seat's opened page** - when a seat opens a page, a card names the seat and the link with its query hidden, since a login's state rides there. **Show here** switches to that seat, shows its Browser tab, takes control of its browser and loads the page, and the seat's own browser use waits until it is handed back. **Not now** clears it, and the screen never moves on its own. A repeat of one link is one card, and others wait behind the newest. A host is subscribed only if it lists `open-url` and `browser`, so any other host opens the page in its own browser ([open-url.md](open-url.md)).
* **Peer message marking** - envelope lines are matched in the terminal buffer and striped in the sender's colour, across wrapped rows.
* **Messages** - each seat's sent and received messages with queued, held, launching, typed in, and failed states, and the daemon's reason. A row opens in place to the message's full text and closes again, by tap or Enter and Space. Opening only reads, so nothing is answered. The text shows only when the daemon sends it, which it does to a client that may type, and without it the row says so. A cut message says so too (COI-3362, daemon half [message-text.md](message-text.md)).
* **Pages for you** - a seat's registered claude.ai page (`aterm artifact add`) is listed at the top of the seat overview and every Views tab, waiting ones first, and opens in a reader that draws the seat's own source in a script-only sandbox with Open on claude.ai first. A waiting page's toast opens it. [artifacts.md](artifacts.md) (COI-3330).
* **Views and Browser tabs** - MCP Apps views from a daemon that lists `mcp-apps`, and the seat's streamed browser, awaiting its frames. [views-and-browser.md](views-and-browser.md).
* **Phone layout** - below 720px one switcher bar names the seat and holds the seats, roles to start, hosts, and Settings. The sheet lists seats first with their state and role on one line, roles to start as one sideways row of chips, and hosts and Settings folded under one summary row, and it runs to the bottom edge over the tab strip. At 412 by 800 the box shows 30 rows with the keyboard closed and 16 open, with one text input. [architecture.md](architecture.md) says how the harness's own box is hidden.
* **Activity line** - a seat row, the phone sheet and an overview row show the one line a seat wrote about what it is doing (`activity` and `activity_at`, [seat-state.md](seat-state.md)), cut with an ellipsis to the width that fits: about 33 characters in the desktop sidebar and 42 at 320px. Past 5 minutes the text mutes and its age ("3h ago") stays whole at the right. A seat that sent none, or a line with no usable time, shows nothing and the row is unchanged. A chip grows to 190px for the line and no further, so the next chip still shows at the edge at 320px (COI-3340).
* **Activity line in the top bar** - on a screen wide enough for it, the open seat's top bar carries the same line under its name, one line, whole on hover, flagged once old or once it predates the seat's present state (`state_seconds`), and gone when the seat set none. A phone's bar keeps its one row so the terminal keeps every row (COI-3519).
* **Landscape reading view** - a coarse-pointer screen under 500px tall, turned on its side with a seat open, folds the list and header into a 52px left rail. The rail button opens the seats, roles, hosts and Settings as a full-height drawer beside it. Rotating back restores the portrait bar. The side panel stays a bottom strip until COI-3324 makes it an overlay, and the composer still takes about 110px (COI-3341).
* **PTY size** - when the daemon lists `pty-size`, a terminal reports its box's capacity (`fit.proposeDimensions()`) with `scales: true` on attach, and sets xterm to exactly the `size` frame the daemon answers with. A PTY larger than the box pans, anchored bottom-left, so the input and newest text stay in view. An older daemon keeps fitting and sending.
* **Terminal text size** - on a terminal under 360px wide, and at any size but 14 so the way back stays, an Aa button at its top right opens a Smaller text, size and Larger text row, each a 44px target, reachable by keyboard, with Escape to close. Steps are 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16 and 18px, 14 by default as before, kept on the device and read again on reload. A smaller size fits more columns, which the client reports as its box like any resize, so the button and the Wrap switch wait while a choice card covers the terminal (COI-3574). [sizing.md](sizing.md) (COI-3481).
* **Focus, Default and Verbose views** - on a host whose welcome lists `transcript`, a three-stop Seat view above the seat reads its transcript as prompts, replies and tool lines. Focus is the phone default, peer messages never show in it, and Verbose has a Raw screen button. [seat-views.md](seat-views.md) (COI-3532).
* **Media viewer** - a seat's shared images and video open in a viewer over the seat, with a chip above the composer, Rotate for an upside-down frame, Previous and Next through a sequence, and a Download card for any other file. Needs a host listing `media`. [media.md](media.md) (COI-3458).
* **Wrap long lines** - on a touch screen or a window under 721px wide, while the PTY is wider than the box (or the switch is on), a Wrap button at the terminal's top right (a 44px target, `aria-pressed`) folds a wide PTY's rows to the box's width, one seat at a time, kept on the device. A device default in Settings (COI-3612) folds a seat on its own once it overflows: Auto on a phone or narrow window and not on a desktop, or On, or Off. The button overrides it for that seat, and an Off press is kept too (COI-3317). The fold is the client's alone and sends the daemon nothing, so a desktop view of the seat does not move. A tap still opens a link in a folded row, and an envelope's stripe runs down every folded line of its row (COI-3564). [sizing.md](sizing.md) (COI-3544).
* **Read-only state** - when the daemon's typing guard refuses this browser (`welcome.typing`, or an `error` with `session_descendant` or `peer_unread`), the terminal, composer, choice cards, and launch buttons say read only and send nothing.
* **Passkey unlock** - a locked remote device enrolls and asserts a passkey from the hosted build, then types. [passkey.md](passkey.md).
* **Terminal tab and split** - a plain shell beside a seat, a resizable split, and the side panel opening on a tab by role (sysadmin Terminal, frontend Browser). On a desktop the side panel starts put away, so the seat's terminal has the width. A Side panel button in the seat's header opens it (with a count of messages), and the choice is kept per browser. Below 1000px it stays a bottom sheet that starts put away (COI-3238). [terminal-split.md](terminal-split.md).
* **Hosted build** - the client under a path prefix behind a deployment's own sign-in, hosts added per device. [deploy.md](deploy.md).
* **Installable** - a manifest, icons, and an app-shell service worker, so Chrome installs it from the daemon's address or the hosted build into its own window. With no daemon answering, a launch opens to the "not answering" panel instead of a browser error. An install card on the host panel says what installing does and hides once installed. [deploy.md](deploy.md).

Designed, not built: the switchboard.
