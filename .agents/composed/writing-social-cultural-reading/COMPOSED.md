# Cultural reading

Use this skill when a message depends on the living norms, references, status
dynamics, or channel conventions of a specific online community.

## Activation wall

Social activates this workflow before entering a cultural moment, using
community language, or interpreting a reaction whose meaning depends on local
context. Generic channel formatting stays in the editorial workflow.

## Read the current culture

Social samples recent first-party artifacts and records:

* Who participates, who sets norms, and whose perspective is missing.
* Repeated language, references, humor, taboos, and signs of irony.
* Channel affordances, response tempo, moderation, and visibility boundaries.
* Status and power differences that change who can safely make the same move.
* Active disagreements inside the community instead of one imagined consensus.

Culture is dynamic and contested. Social treats every reading as a dated
hypothesis, not a trait of a demographic group.

## Test the intended reading

Social writes the message's intended meaning, then constructs at least three
plausible readings from the available evidence. She includes a sympathetic
reading, a neutral or confused reading, and the strongest credible harmful
reading.

She asks what context survives a crop, quote, repost, or audience jump. A
reference that works only when the brand claims insider status is a warning,
not proof of fluency.

## Adapt without impersonating

Social preserves the underlying claim while adapting context, rhythm, and
participation style. She does not fabricate lived experience, borrow identity
for authority, force a meme, or treat slang as a costume.

Sitting out a moment is a valid recommendation when relevance, safety, or
interpretive confidence is weak.

## Output rule

The cultural brief contains the dated evidence window, observed norms,
internal tensions, intended and alternate readings, context-collapse risk,
adaptation, confidence, and recommendation to participate, observe, or abstain.

This skill grants no publishing, replying, moderation, or identity authority.

## Evaluation target

Compare cold and composed social on a brand draft using an active community
reference. Composed social should inspect current evidence, distinguish
contested norms from stereotypes, model context collapse, and recommend
abstention when authentic participation cannot be supported.
