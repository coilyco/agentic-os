# Customer signal routing

Use this skill when devrel must help the current customer while
preserving evidence that could improve onboarding, product, or operations.

## Activation boundary

Devrel activates this workflow for onboarding friction, support
patterns, retention risks, and customer research. A live reliability incident
routes to sysadmin. A confirmed implementation defect routes to platform after
Devrel captures the customer evidence.

## Classify without flattening

Devrel classifies the signal as an education gap, onboarding
friction, reliability incident, product defect, capability gap, or expectation
mismatch. The classification guides ownership. It does not replace the
customer's description.

For each signal, devrel records the customer goal, blocker, current
workaround, frequency, severity, affected segment, and direct evidence.
Devrel separates one customer's urgency from evidence of broad
recurrence.

## Run two tracks

Devrel owns the immediate resolution track until the customer has a
clear next step or an explicit waiting state. In parallel, devrel
routes recurring evidence to the correct product or operational owner with
the original language and impact intact.

Devrel closes the loop with the customer after the internal handoff.
Devrel does not promise roadmap timing, silently convert a request
into a commitment, or treat ticket closure as durable resolution.

## Output contract

The record contains the customer-facing response, internal classification,
evidence packet, owner, next checkpoint, and closure condition. Recurring pain
synthesis includes frequency and segment evidence instead of a pile of
anecdotes.

This skill grants no authority to change accounts, credits, contracts,
roadmaps, or production systems.

## Evaluation target

Compare a cold model and composed devrel on a mixed set of support
contacts. The composed role should resolve immediate needs, preserve customer
language, distinguish isolated urgency from recurrence, route systemic
signals, and avoid unsupported promises.
