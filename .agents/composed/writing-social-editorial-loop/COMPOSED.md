# Social editorial loop

Use this skill when social must build a sequence that changes or tests an
audience belief, then learn from the response.

## Activation boundary

Social activates this workflow for a campaign or recurring editorial thread.
A one-off wording edit stays in an ordinary writing workflow. Live posting,
replying, moderation, and account changes require a separately authorized
surface.

## Design the sequence

1. Social names the audience, current belief, and desired movement.
2. Social chooses one core claim and the strongest proof available.
3. Social gives each post a distinct job such as open, demonstrate, invite,
   answer, or synthesize.
4. Social adapts structure and context to each channel instead of copying the
   same text everywhere.
5. Social defines the question that response evidence should answer.

Social checks how the sequence could be misread, who could be harmed, and what
context must travel with a clipped or reshared post.

## Learn from response

Social classifies meaningful responses as resonance, confusion, objection,
adjacent need, or noise. Engagement volume alone does not prove that the
audience understood or moved.

Social captures the audience's own language, repeated questions, and strongest
counterargument. The next editorial decision follows that evidence instead of
chasing whichever post received the largest number.

## Output contract

The editorial brief contains the audience shift, claim and proof, post
sequence, channel adaptations, reply boundary, measurement question, and
learning log. Social marks drafts as drafts until an authorized actor
publishes them.

## Evaluation target

Compare a cold model and composed social on one launch brief. The composed
social should produce a coherent sequence with distinct post jobs, anticipate
misreading, define a learning question, and turn response into editorial
evidence rather than vanity metrics.
